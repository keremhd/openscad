# D13 — the hole is not at the junction, and what four ways of closing it cost

D13 was written up as a junction defect: two bosses overlap, three creases meet
at each end of their seam, the tool comes back holed, and the three places to
look were all inside the corner machinery. None of them is where the hole is.

## The hole is on one boss, with no junction anywhere near it

Cut the model down to **one** boss and the hole is still there:

```openscad
$fn = 48;
module boss() { cube([60, 40, 6]); translate([38, 20, 6]) cylinder(r = 10, h = 16); }
fillet_tool(r = 2) boss();
```

genus 2, where a base ring on its own is genus 1. The tool has one closed chain,
no junction, no corner cell, no truncation and no runout — every mechanism D13
pointed at is switched off, and the defect is unchanged. Move the boss to
`x = 22`, `25` or `30` and it goes away; leave it at 38 and change `$fn` or `r`
and it comes and goes:

| `$fn` | r=1 | r=2 | r=3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16, 24, 32 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 48 | 1 | **2** | 1 |
| 64 | **2** | 1 | **2** |
| 96, 128 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

That is the same "not a function of the shape" the two-boss table showed, and it
is the whole of it: on the two-boss model at `dx = 16` the extra handle is at
**(43.4, 29.4)** — on the far boss's base ring, 15 mm from either junction.

## What it looks like

At one station of the ring the bead has a **slit** through it: a lens-shaped gash
a few tenths of a millimetre long, lying in that station's own section plane,
with a dozen near-duplicate vertices along it. Not a gap between beads, not a
corner that failed to close — a hole punched through an otherwise ordinary
stretch of bead.

Both halves are sound going in. Clipped to a box around it, the wedge union is
genus 0 and the canal union is genus 0; the slit appears in `wedge - canal`.

## Why it happens

`appendChainCells` cuts a chain into one cell per segment, so consecutive cells
**abut on a shared face**, and that face is always the plane of a station. The
wedges and the canals are built along the same chain by the same routine, so the
two unions carry their seams in the *same* planes — the wedge's pentagon and the
canal's disc, coplanar and overlapping.

`unionCells` does not clear those faces away: on the boss at `$fn = 48` the
canal union comes back with 54 vertex positions carrying more than one vertex,
one or two per station. The subtraction then has to resolve a pair of coincident
faces at every station of every chain, and where the canal's surface also grazes
that plane it comes down on the wrong side. Whether it does is a matter of which
way the arithmetic falls, which is why the answer moves with `$fn`, with `r` and
with where the boss is standing.

## Four ways of closing it, and what each costs

All four remove the coincidence. The measurements are the boss above across
`$fn` 16–128 and `r` 1–3, the D13 repro at `dx` 0/12/16/19, the unit tests,
`ctest -R fillet|chamfer|bevel|round`, `fillet-tests/run_all.sh`, and a
two-boss union at `$fn = 128` for time.

### A. Run each canal cell a hair past its station, along its own ruling

`tail = lerp(a, b, 1 + 1e-4)`. Fixes every genus above and every D13
configuration. Fails **"curved wall: the blend leaves no crease the model does
not have"**: the extrapolated tip stands outside the next cell on the outside of
the bend by an amount that goes as the overrun, and its flat end cap leaves a
nick whose *angle* — 99.8 degrees — does not shrink with it. Same reading at
1e-2, 1e-4 and 1e-5; at 1e-6 the grazing is back.

### B. Cut the canal at half-integers instead

Cells from mid-segment to mid-segment, so no seam is in a station's plane. The
canal then no longer touches the arc at any station, the wedge's chord face
there is cut away by nothing, and the tool comes back in 67 pieces. Giving each
cell the station's own section as a third face fixes the tangency and is exact
on the outer side — but a hull is convex, so on the *inner* side of every bend
it fills the notch between the two rulings, which is a real change of shape:
19 of 77 ctest cases fail.

### C. One extra cell straddling each seam

`hull(section(k - d), a_k, section(k + d))`, `d` a thousandth of a segment,
skipped where three consecutive sections lie in a line and capped at a 45-degree
turn (without the cap the rib's right-angled base corners take a hundredth of a
millimetre out of each corner and the size gate refuses the rib's own creases).
Unit tests and ctest both green, `run_all.sh` turns `case_two_bosses` and both
`case_dome_on_plate` variants from FAIL to PASS. Time drops from 1.38 s to
0.89 s.

What it costs is the mesh. The cover's outer surface is exactly flush with the
cells it bridges, so the union keeps its two end rings — a thousandth of a
segment either side of every station — and the bead comes back with **twice the
triangles** and, on the pipe tee, 16 of them with no area. `case_pipe_tee_fillet
large sandwich` goes from PASS to FAIL: CGAL will not dilate the result.

### D. The same cover, drawn in off its own sections

Inset each of the cover's sections toward its own middle by twice the departure
measured at the seam, so the cover sits inside the two cells it bridges and
contributes no surface at all. This is the one that is right on paper: the tool
comes back **byte-identical in size** to today's (321 vertices on
`fillet-tests.scad`'s boss, against 321), the pipe tee is identical to today at
both radii with no degenerate faces, and every genus above is 1.

Two things are wrong with it. The cover still reaches the boundary at four spots
on that boss — 11 vertices, and 2 more pinch vertices than before, at a shape
whose volume is unchanged to every digit, so it is retriangulation rather than a
change of surface. And CGAL's Nef trips an assertion in `SNC_external_structure`
unioning the four models of `fillet-tests.scad`, though each of the four on its
own, and every pair of them, converts fine. Raising the inset by 2x, 8x, 16x
does not move either number; shrinking the reach to 1e-5 clears the assertion
and adds four zero-area faces instead. That is the same coin-toss the defect is,
one level down.

Time is the other cost: 2.4x, because every seam adds a cell that genuinely
overlaps two others rather than resting flush against them.

## What the shape of the problem says

A cell has to be cut **at** a station, because the section's walls turn there and
a hull spanning a turn fills the notch on its inner side (that is B). Both unions
are built from the same stations, so both are cut in the same planes, and the
coincidence is not something the partition can be rearranged out of. Anything
that covers a seam has to be a convex cell laid over a place where the union is
not convex — so it either stands proud of what it bridges (A), or rests flush on
it (C), or is drawn inside it (D), and each of those is paid for somewhere.

The one shape that has none of these problems is a canal built from **balls**
rather than from planar sections: consecutive cells would then overlap in a whole
ball instead of meeting on a disc, and there would be no shared face to resolve
anywhere. It is also the more faithful swept volume — today's planar hulls are
inscribed in it. What it is not is a small change: the sections carry the two
points past each wall that keep the arc from grazing the wedge's wall faces, and
the flat cap square to the spine that a brush boundary needs, and both of those
are properties of a planar section. That is the route to price next.

## What this says about D14 — and what D14 has backwards

D14 files the memory as a defect and names its fix: "the canal's own overlap
trick, declined for the wedges on the grounds that their shared faces cost
nothing but the shells this helper sweeps up". Two things are wrong with that.
There is no overlap trick in the file — `kCanalOverlap` does not exist. And a
cell that overlaps its neighbour **does not stop the shells**. Counted on the
16-boss grid at `$fn = 32`, `Decompose()` over the canal union returns:

| | parts |
|---|---|
| today | 544 |
| C, flush cover per seam | **661** |
| D, inset cover per seam | **1339** |

Roughly one per station either way — the covers add their own contacts faster
than they remove the ones they were laid over. Whatever closes D13 will not
close D14 as a side effect, and the first of D14's two routes should be struck.

### The helper is the whole of it, and it is nearly independent of D13

D14's grid, `fillet_tool(r = 1)` at `$fn = 32`, peak RSS:

| bosses | today | C | C on wedges too | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 0.32 GB | 0.40 | 0.39 | 0.46 |
| 36 | 2.45 | 1.31 | 1.32 | 1.43 |
| 64 | **11.40** | **4.49** | 6.32 | **14.11** |

C is the only one that helps — 2.5x at 64 bosses — and it does not linearise
anything: the exponent goes from 2.7 to 2.1. D is worse than doing nothing.

Now the same grid with `dropVolumelessParts` returning its argument untouched,
which is the bound D14's second route is aiming at:

| bosses | today | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 0.68 s / 0.18 GB | 0.86 / 0.28 | 1.12 / 0.26 |
| 36 | 0.58 / 0.33 | 1.04 / 0.42 | 0.91 / 0.36 |
| 64 | **1.01 s / 0.44 GB** | **1.79 / 0.59** | 1.62 / 0.53 |

11.40 GB to 0.44, 10.72 s to 1.01, and linear in the boss count for all three.
**The memory is not the builder's, and it is not D13's to fix** — it is one
helper, and D14's union-find route removes it whatever lands here. What the D13
routes then cost is the extra cells and nothing more: C is +34% memory and 1.8x
time over doing nothing, both linear.

So the order is: fix `dropVolumelessParts` first, on its own, since it is
independent and worth 26x memory by itself; then choose between C and D on the
mesh and the shape, which is where they actually differ, rather than on a cost
that belongs to neither.

## Also true, and separate

- `dx = 19` and above still gives the bare tool genus 5 and 607 mm³ where twice
  one boss is 114. The *union* is genus 0 there once the slit is closed, at
  every `dx` and both tessellations, because the excess is corner-cell hull
  spilling into the boss rather than material outside it. Still worth its own
  entry: at a nearly-tangent crossing the corner cell's hull spans most of the
  lens between the two cylinders.
- The two junctions of the D13 model do have the spiky, unclosed look the
  write-up describes. They are not holes: with the slit closed, the union is
  genus 0 at `dx` 0, 12, 16 and 19 and at `$fn` 48 and 96, and cutting either
  junction out of the tool leaves the rest sound.
