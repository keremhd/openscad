# 2026-07-29 — T2: the curved junction, and a dome that never runs out

Two cases built, `case_two_bosses` and `case_dome_on_plate`. Both went in
green-where-they-can-be and red where the harness cannot reach; what is worth
recording is that three of the four things they were written to check came out
differently from the prediction.

## The junction is real, and the rings do not survive

Two bosses of radius 10 on a plate, centres 14 apart, so they overlap by 6 of
their 20 diameter and the groove between them is a 91-degree crease rather than
a tangency. That is the whole design of the shape: `case_pipe_tee_equal` failed
to supply a curved junction because equal radii are what make two seam loops
cross *and* what makes the two cylinders share a tangent plane where they do, so
the dihedral is under the threshold before the crossing arrives. Creases have to
meet at an angle, and two bosses can do that where a tee cannot at any radius.

Measured: the two closed base rings are each cut at both crossings into a single
open arc running round the outside of its own boss, so four chains come back —
two curved, two straight — and each crossing carries three chain ends. Three
walls, one seated ball, one corner cell, at `r = 1`, `2` and `4` alike. No
crease refused, no runout warning, and the tool is one connected piece of
genus 1, which is exactly what the topology demands: the two arcs close into a
loop through the two corners.

The section was watching for the rings *not* surviving as rings. They do not,
and that is the correct answer rather than the failure.

## The corner cell sheds a crumb, and it is D2's remainder on a new shape

The case was drafted with the bosses at `$fn = 48`. At 48 the tool comes back as
**three** pieces: the right one, plus a detached wafer at each junction —
0.076 x 0.061 x 0.005, 5.2e-06 of volume — sitting on the plate face at the
point where the two ring beads' outer edges cross, which is one radius outside
both rings. Its z span is 5.997 to 6.002 on a plate whose top is at 6: the
wedge's own eps overshoot, shed by the corner cell.

That is the same geometry the pocket cases are permanently red for, and the
first time it has been seen anywhere but on a spike. It says the open item is
not about a corner cell hulled against a barely turning bead — an ordinary
right-angled junction on a curved crease reaches it too. Left alone, for D2's
stated reason, with the reproduction written down.

The case is written at `$fn = 24`, where the tool is one piece and the genus is
right. A 24-gon turns 15 degrees a facet against a 22.5-degree threshold, so the
ring is a curve to the classifier either way, and the case keeps its question.
The crumb is recorded in the case header and in `next-cases.md` rather than
pinned as correct.

## The dome never runs out of constant-radius solutions

The dome variant was queued to show "both the size that fits and the size that
cannot", on the theory that a blend whose radius approaches the curvature it
follows has no constant-radius solution.

It has one at every size. On a plate wide enough, an `R = 8` dome takes `r = 30`
without a warning, and correctly: the crease is a circle in the plate's own
plane, and the ball seated in it meets the plate one radius outside that circle,
which always exists. The tangency on the dome climbs toward the pole and never
leaves the hemisphere.

What refuses the oversize variant is the **plate**. The crease's radius is
7.9829 — the 48-gon equator's inscribed one — and the plate reaches 20 from the
axis, so the contact leaves it at `r = 12.017`, and above that the overshoot the
warning reports is `r - 12.017` to five figures at every size probed. The
refusal is the plate's arithmetic and nothing else's.

That is a stronger statement about D6 than the case was written to make: the
dome's curvature is measured to cost the gate *nothing*, which is what "stop
constructing the contact point and ask the wall" was supposed to buy.

## Neither case can be given a `sandwich` verdict

CGAL refuses to dilate either applied result. Controls, because the tee's line
in `expectations.txt` is already one of these and a second unexplained one would
be worthless:

- one boss on the same plate dilates at `$fn = 24` and refuses at 48 — no
  junction in it either way;
- two bosses dilate at `$fn = 12` and refuse at 16, 24 and 48;
- radius makes no difference (1, 2, 3, 4 all refuse) and neither does the clip,
  whole model or a box around one junction alone;
- the dome refuses down to `$fn = 16`, which is coarser than the shape is about,
  so no version of that case both keeps its question and passes.

So the ceiling is how much filleted curved surface the Nef conversion has to
swallow, not a junction and not either solid. It is the harness gap
`next-cases.md` already names: `sandwich` cannot see a tangency, because CGAL
will not dilate a result whose surfaces meet tangentially, and a fillet meeting
its wall is exactly that.

The dome's oversize variant is unaffected — an empty tool leaves `sandwich`
comparing the model with itself — and passes, along with its `drops`.

## Tests

`FilletBuilder_test.cc` gains "chains: two curved creases and a straight one meet
at a real junction": four open chains, both ends of every one on the crossing
line, two of them single-segment grooves, and two valence-3 junctions each with
three walls and one seated ball at three radii.

"size: a wall's own curvature is not an overshoot" gains the far end of the same
axis: the dome fits at `r = 10`, is refused at 14 and 18, and the overshoot
grows with the radius — the plate's limit, stated where it is cheap.

`run_all.sh`: 82 checks, all matching expectations.
