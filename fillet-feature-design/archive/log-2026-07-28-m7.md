# 2026-07-28 — M7: the rounded tools

`fillet_tool` and `round_tool` had been parsing their arguments and emitting
nothing since M0. They now build a solid: the wedge the chamfer already builds,
with the rolling ball's canal taken back out of it.

## What landed

`roundSections` and `buildRoundSolid` in `fillet::detail`, plus a `concave` flag
on `spineFrames`. Two `.scad` cases under `tests/data/scad/3D/features/` with
committed baselines, and four comparisons against hand-written references in
`FilletCompare_test.cc` — the inner corner, one cube edge, the hole mouth, and
the boss base at a radius twice the boss's own.

The setback is not a parameter any more. For the wedge tools it is what the
caller asked for; for the rounded ones it is `r·tan(φ/2)` at each station, which
is where a ball of radius `r` seated in the crease touches each wall. So it
varies along a chain wherever the wall angle does, instead of being one number
for the whole chain, and the triangle `(v, TA, TB)` is exactly the corner the arc
is inscribed in.

## Three things the plan did not say

**The frame has a sign, and nothing had ever asked it for one.** `spineFrames`
put the ball centre at `v + d·bis` and the tangency points at `C − r·n`. That is
right for a concave crease, where both outward normals point into the empty
quadrant. At a convex one the ball sits inside the solid and all three signs
flip. Nothing caught it at M4 because the only reader was the debug overlay,
which draws whatever it is given.

**The section plane comes from the normals, not from the spine.** §6.1 says the
tangency points lie in the plane perpendicular to the spine "since both are
offsets from `C` along face normals, which are perpendicular to the spine". That
holds on a straight edge. Around a bend the averaged normals and the local spine
direction stop agreeing, and only the plane spanned by `nA` and `nB` still
contains `C`, `TA` and `TB` by construction. The boss-base case is the one that
says so out loud: its fillet is twice the radius of the boss it sits on, and
taking the plane from the spine walks the bead off the axis.

**`W − U` is per chain, not global.** This is the one that produced visible
damage. Subtract the union of every chain's canal from the union of every
chain's wedge and, at a cube corner, the ball rolling along one edge reaches past
the meeting point into the neighbouring edge's wedge — and removes exactly the
material that neighbour is there to remove. The cube came out with a lump on all
eight corners and its faces eaten back into a clover shape. Within a chain the
subtraction still has to be global rather than cell-by-cell, since a cell's ball
does reach into the next cell's wedge wherever the spine bends. Between chains it
must not. What belongs at a meeting point is a corner cell (§6.3), and until
there is one the honest answer is to let the beads overlap, which is what the
wedge tools already do.

A probe test pins it: a small box that sits in the top edge's bead and outside
that bead's own ball, but inside the *vertical* edge's ball. It is tool whatever
happens at the corner, and a global subtraction removes it.

## The circular-segment optimization was implemented, measured, and backed out

§6.1 asks for discs first and circular segments after, "output unchanged within
tolerance". Both were written. The segment version is correct — every reference
comparison passes — and it is smaller: 2366 → 2087 vertices on the hole mouth,
and 26 hull input points a cell instead of 96.

It is also not a shape CGAL will take. The segment's chord *is* the wedge's own
outer face, so `W − U` asks the boolean to resolve two exactly coincident
surfaces along the entire length of the tool, and both closed-ring cases stopped
being dilatable — `minkowski()` on them fails in the Nef convex decomposition,
where the disc version is fine.

The obvious fix is the one the pentagon already uses on the walls: push the chord
a hair past, so the surfaces cross instead of coinciding. The bisector is
perpendicular to the chord, so this stays convex and costs two points. It works
on the hole mouth at 2 % of `r` — twenty times the `eps` the walls use — and
still does not work on the boss base at any margin tried.

And the margin is not free to choose. Between two stations the hull of the
pentagons overhangs each station's chord plane, by an amount that depends on how
the spine turns — about 0.3 mm on the boss base at `$fn = 48`. That overhang is
material the ball genuinely covers and the segment genuinely misses, so the strip
has to be at least that deep and no deeper, which is a per-cell computation, not
a constant. The full disc gets it right for free, because the major segment it
carries *is* the rest of the ball.

So: discs stay. The saving is 12 % of the vertices of a solid that is already
small, and it is not worth a tool that downstream CGAL cannot process. The
question comes back at M8, where junction cells put spheres against these same
faces.

## What the corner looks like now

A cube rounded on all twelve edges, with the corners left as they are — three
beads meeting in a small facet. Incomplete, which is what M7 said it would be,
rather than wrong. The 3-face cone apex is the one place that shows up as a
failing check: the three balls between them cut the tip off, and the result
strays further from the model than the radius. That is the corner cell's job.

## Test-instrument fixes that came out of it

Two, both in `FilletCompare_test.cc`, both cases of the instrument being wrong
rather than the operator:

- The residual between two solids that agree exactly is geometrically nothing,
  but Manifold leaves a volumeless four-triangle shell wherever a boolean's
  operands touch on a set of zero measure — which is what exact agreement looks
  like. `IsEmpty()` said false. The verdict is now "no component encloses
  material", at a cutoff nine orders below the solids being compared.
- The hand-written references were tessellating at Manifold's default of 12
  segments a circle, whose chord error is larger than the 2 % tolerance the cases
  compare at. A reference coarser than the tolerance fails a correct tool.
