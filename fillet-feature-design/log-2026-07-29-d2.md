# 2026-07-29 — D2: the tool's own tangency strip

`round_tool(r=2) cube();` under `--backend=cgal` rendered with facets dropped,
and two CTests were disabled for it. Fixed. The diagnosis the item was written
with was wrong in both halves, which is most of what is worth recording.

## It was not the corner spheres, and nothing was being quantised

The written cause was that a sphere sits tangent to three walls at every corner
and leaves triangles too small to survive CGAL quantising its input. CGAL's
kernel is exact and quantises nothing; what it does is throw
`Assertion_exception` out of the `Nef_polyhedron_3` constructor when a facet
encloses no area, and OpenSCAD then falls back to unioning the faces one at a
time and reports what it had to discard. So the question is not how small a
triangle is, it is whether it is *degenerate*, and that is a yes/no a test can
ask.

Asked of the cube's tool: 25 degenerate triangles, and they were spread along
the creases, not gathered at the corners. Asked of a cylinder's tool — closed
chains at both rims, therefore no junctions at all — none, and it had converted
cleanly the whole time. The corners were where the count was highest, not where
the cause was.

## The cause

Two pieces of the tool stop at different distances from the same wall:

- the **wedge** stands `eps` past it, deliberately, so the caller's boolean has
  a transversal face to cut rather than a coincident one;
- the **arc** is tangent to it, so it reaches the wall exactly and no further.

Between them, along the whole crease, sits a strip of the wedge's own overshoot:
`eps` thick, and running out to nothing at either end where the arc curves away.
That is the sliver. It is the gap between a tangent and its tangent plane, so
more arc segments only make it thinner — which is why "phase the tessellation",
the approach the item proposed, cannot reach it. Junctions matter because the
corner cells cut the crease short and drop extra vertices onto the strip's long
edge; three collinear points on an edge that thin is a triangle with no area.

## The ladder

Every piece now stands further past a wall than the piece it must cut through:

| | past each wall |
|---|---|
| wedge cells | `eps` |
| corner cells | `1.5 eps` |
| the subtracted arc | `2 eps` |

The arc's overshoot is two extra hull points per section, in the two tangency
directions. The arc is otherwise untouched — it is not a larger ball, which is
the alternative the item rejected for good reason, and the blend still meets
each wall where a ball of exactly `r` touches it to within the overshoot the
tool already carried there.

The corner cell needed two changes to fit. Its overshoot had been set to *half*
the wedge's on the argument that matching it would put two faces in one plane;
being under it is the worse of the two, because the face then grazes just
beneath rather than crossing. And its vertex point was displaced once along the
bisector of every wall at the junction, which lands short of each wall plane by
the cosine — enough to tilt the cell's entire wall face. One copy of the vertex
per wall, each along its own normal, and the face is that wall's plane exactly.

That tilt had a second symptom nobody had attributed to it: a flake of the model
left unblended along every junction, whose thickness scaled with the overshoot.
It was sitting just under the threshold of the exact comparisons — raising the
overshoot alone pushed three of them red, which is how it was found.

## What it did not fix

`case_junction3_pocket` and `case_junction4_pocket` are still red and their
ledger entry is rewritten rather than deleted. Their tools now contain no
degenerate triangle at all and CGAL still refuses them, over nine triangles of
about 1e-13 at the one height on each slant bead where it is cut back for the
apex corner. A second tangency, and a different one: the corner cell is hulled
against a section of the bead it closes, so on a spike — one segment from base
to apex, walls that barely turn — its faces leave the bead's at a fraction of a
degree. Probing the separation the cell is hulled at moves the count around
without finding a floor, which says shape rather than constant. It is the same
geometry the apex `sandwich` lines are already permanently red for, and it wants
the corner cell rethought. Left alone.

## Re-fillet, re-measured

T1 asked for this if D2 landed. Reading back a rounded cube gives 480 features
and 288 spurious concave ones where it gave 650 and 383, and the shortest edge a
spurious crease sits on doubles, 6.6e-4 to 1.3e-3 on a 40 mm part. A quarter of
the noise was the tool's own strip. The rest is not the tool's to remove: in the
*applied* result the bead really does meet the flat face tangentially, which is
what a fillet is. `min_angle=` remains the answer that ships for re-reading one.

## Tests

`FilletCompare_test.cc` gains one case: the rounded tools, both signs, leave no
triangle without area. It fails on the code before this (8 on the cube) and is
the whole defect in one number, without needing CGAL in the unit-test build. The
two CTests are re-enabled and their `render-cgal` baseline regenerated; every
other tool baseline is unchanged.
