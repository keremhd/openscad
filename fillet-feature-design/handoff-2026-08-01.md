## Standards the owner set — apply these to every remaining decision

1. **A defect is real only if refining `$fn` does not fix it.** Patchiness that
   shrinks with refinement is faceting and is acceptable. An error that stays at
   `$fn = 192` is a modelling mistake.
2. **Practical and visual, not mathematically pure.** The feature exists to make
   printable, good-looking models. "Self-touching = 0" is a useful proxy, not an
   absolute; we do not need the last mile.
3. **A visible line where two roundings meet is fine.** The bar is a *valid solid*,
   not a smooth corner.
4. **Generalisation over patchwork.** A generic solver that works on everything
   beats special cases whose quality jumps on small user changes. Being bad but
   uniform beats being excellent but discontinuous.
5. **Do not reverse-engineer curvature from the tessellation.** OpenSCAD does not
   have curved surfaces and users do not expect it to.
6. **Judge by maintenance cost, not development effort.** 790 lines for a 10 %
   improvement is a bad trade unless the code is cleanly isolatable.
7. **No user option for wall fitting** — decided. `plane + cylinder`, always on.

## D18 — THREE ROUTES CLOSED, AND A LAW THAT PREDICTS ANY FOURTH

**Read this before touching D18 again.** The root cause is `wallOvershoot`'s sagitta
term, not the seating — established by two controls:

- **Better seating alone changes nothing.** Refine the ball centre against the real
  walls but keep shipping's normals: tee 13.32 / 10.20 / **9.86** at `$fn` 48/96/192
  — identical to shipping.
- **The overshoot alone is the whole defect.** Leave shipping's seating untouched and
  drop only the sagitta term: **5.73 / 3.74 / 1.87**, converging *below* the 3.62
  control.

`wallOvershoot` adds the wall's sagitta (`r²/2R` ≈ 0.2 at r=2, R=5) so the tool's
flat face crosses a curved wall rather than resting on it. The arc's **outriggers**
carry the blend that far past tangency. It does not refine away because the sagitta
is a property of the shape; the flat-wall control is exempt because a prism facet is
flat.

### The law

**`median crease = acos(r / (r + X))`**, where `X` is how far past the ball's
tangency plane the outrigger reaches. Measured to within **0.5° every time**. Use it
to judge any proposed fix *before building it*.

### Why the outrigger route cannot reach the control

`X ≥ f` is **forced**: the wedge's corner `TA` sits in the tangency plane at `v`, and
the wall is `f` (the shape's own sagitta) below it there, so the tool has an `f`-tall
face to get past before it is inside the model. The best conceivable outrigger — on
the wall directly under `TA` — gives `acos(r/(r+f))` ≈ **8.5° at `$fn`=48, 7.4° at
192**, against a 3.62° control. Reaching the control needs `X ≤ 0.008`, an order
below the ledge. Measured coefficients:

| outrigger | X | predicted | tee 48/96/192 | Steinmetz (convex) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `2(f+h)` shipping | .048 | 12.5° | 12.62 / 10.12 / 9.84 | genus 0 |
| `1.25f+2h` | .032 | 10.1° | 9.87 / 8.14 / 7.81 | **genus 1** |
| `f+2h` (wall-shaped) | .026 | 9.2° | 9.34 / 7.30 / 7.06 | **genus 1** |

**The 2× sagitta is load-bearing for convex topology.** *Every* reduction below it
flips the Steinmetz solid's genus at some tessellation, non-monotonically in the
coefficient — a knife edge, not a margin. And "follow the wall" tangential reshaping
is **strictly worse** with a mechanism: the cutting chord rises faster than the wall,
re-emerges before `x = t`, and leaves the wedge's corner uncut as a bare 90° face —
tool-model edges over 45° go 0 → 120 / 172 / 336.

The guard that reddens first is `curved wall: the blend leaves no crease the model
does not have` (`FilletBuilder_test.cc:444`); its safe threshold is
tessellation-dependent (`$fn`=16 needs ≥0.1·f margin, 32 needs ≥0.05·f, 96 needs
none), which is why **no fixed coefficient is safe for arbitrary input**.

### The three routes tried, all closed

| route | size | result |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface recogniser** (plane/cylinder/cone/sphere fit) | 790 lines | converges (4.41°) but concave-only; convex Steinmetz genus 0 → **−72**; sphere fitter misfits a 0.36°-draft boss into a non-converging **159°** crease; only improved things by collapsing the overshoot as a side effect |
| **Facet-plane seating** (no recognition) | 189 lines | converges better (**3.73/4.49**), smooth sweeps, planar bit-exact, avoids *both* recogniser blockers — but **convex regresses** (dish 3.62° → 16.06°), same side-effect mechanism |
| **Outrigger reshaping** | ≤10 lines | **floor of 8.5°/7.4° is forced by `X ≥ f`**; every coefficient below shipping's flips convex genus |

### The only lever left

`f` itself — seating the *section* against the real wall rather than the tangent
plane at `v`. That **moves the centre**, which lands in the pre-existing singularity
below. Anyone attempting it should first predict the outcome with the law above, and
must confront the singularity rather than hope to miss it.

## The equal-radius fold is a PRE-EXISTING singularity, not a new defect

Established by taking **shipping's own seating** (no fit, no frame change, no
overshoot change) and multiplying the ball centre's offset from the crease by a
single scalar:

| | Steinmetz `$fn`=48 r=2 | Steinmetz `$fn`=96 r=1 | equal tee `$fn`=48 |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipping, unnudged | 3 folds, genus 0 | 4 folds (163°) | 0 folds |
| **×1.001 (0.1 %)** | 16 folds, **genus −2** | 12 folds, **180°** | 8 folds, **genus −2** |
| ×1.03 (3 %) | 400 folds | 1099 folds | 608 folds |

**A 0.1 % displacement of shipping's own centre flips genus.** 3 % gives 400–1256
folds — *more* than the surface-recogniser produced. So the folds are a pre-existing
singularity in the tool construction; shipping lands on the lucky side and is fragile
even there (unnudged shipping already shows 3, 4 and 15 folds at three of six
configurations, and `$fn`=50 folds on the standard tee where 52 and 54 are clean).

**Consequence for route selection: any change that moves the seated centre lands in
this regime.** Reshaping the outriggers does not move the centre, so it should be
immune — confirm rather than assume.

## The facet-plane solver — measured, concave-only win

`nearestOnWall` + `seatAgainstWalls`, **189 lines** in `FilletBuilder.cc` (+12 header,
+8 test signatures). No fitting, no recognition, no refusals.

- **Concave converges past the fitted version**: tee b5 4.99/9.25, 4.06/5.79,
  **3.73/4.49** at `$fn` 48/96/192 against the fit's 4.41/4.90 and the 3.62 control.
- **Sweeps are smooth** — branch radius 3→9.5 stays in 3.88–4.19, fillet radius
  0.5→3 in 3.98–4.60, sphere sink 3→20 in 3.93–4.42. **No steps anywhere**, which is
  the uniformity property the fit lacks.
- **Planar bit-for-bit holds, checked on the built solid** — `%a` vertex fingerprints
  unchanged from HEAD for cube-round, L-fillet, boss-on-plate, cone-on-plate. The
  guard is structural: on a plane or prism facet the ball already sits at exactly r,
  so the solver returns at step 0.
- **Both of the fitted version's blockers are avoided**: Steinmetz genus 0 → **0**
  (fit went to −72); equal-radius tee **no 180° folds** either sign, max 86–90°, and
  it converges.
- **Blocker: convex `round_tool` regresses** — tee cavity 3.62 → **6.43**, dish 3.62
  → **16.06** at `$fn`=192, where shipping was clean. The subtracted tool needs the
  overshoot that seating collapses. Measuring the overshoot as the wall's excursion
  above the tool's own face was tried and is a clear negative (convex ~40°, and it
  broke planar bit-exactness).

Worth keeping only if the convex regression is fixed. Scaffolding to revert:
`src/geometry/fillet/FilletTangency_scratch_test.cc` and its line in
`CMakeLists.txt` — that is the measurement harness and is worth keeping until
someone reproduces the numbers. That worktree builds with `-DEXPERIMENTAL=1`;
without it `tests/CMakeLists.txt:1507` fails on an unrelated missing 3MF test.

### The crossing cylinders (the D16 / D18 headline shape)

This is the "pipe tee" every scallop and tangency number is measured on. Matches
`pipeTee()` at `FilletBuilder_test.cc:403` — run radius 10 along z, branch radius
6 along +y at z = 30.

```openscad
$fn = 96;                       // sweep 24 / 48 / 96 / 192
module crossing_pipes(Ra = 10, Rb = 6) {
    union() {
        cylinder(h = 60, r = Ra);
        translate([0, 0, 30]) rotate([-90, 0, 0]) cylinder(h = 25, r = Rb);
    }
}
fillet(r = 2) crossing_pipes();
```

D18 sweeps this as `Rb` ∈ {10, 5, 3} against `Ra` = 10 and `r` ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4}.
`Rb = 3, r = 4` is the worst case and the one in the planar-vs-fitted picture;
`Ra = Rb = 10` is the singular equal-radius case that folds.

### Boss on a plate — the control, where the planar rule is exact
